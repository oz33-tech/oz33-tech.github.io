02

How a proper loft is built

The anatomy of a loft that actually works.

Most "loft boarding" jobs nail chipboard straight onto the joists — crushing the insulation and giving you a bouncy, cold floor. Here's how we do it instead.

  1. i.

    Insulation stays full depth

    Standard boarding squashes insulation by ~50%. Raised legs hold boards above, so the full 270 mm keeps doing its job.

  2. ii.

    No bouncy floor

    Boards spread across the joists, not soft insulation. Walk, load and store up to 350 kg per square metre.

  3. iii.

    No cold spots

    No compressed insulation means no patches where heat escapes. Lower bills, warmer rooms underneath.

  4. iv.

    Building-Regs compliant

    270 mm of mineral wool meets current UK Building Regulations. We top up what's there. No shortcuts.

Common questions

The things people usually ask first.

How long does it take?

A standard loft boarding job is 1–2 days. Adding insulation + ladder + lights pushes it to 2–3 days. I'll give you a real timeline with the quote.

Will boarding squash my insulation?

No — I use a raised-leg system that sits above the joists. Your insulation stays at full depth underneath, doing its job. Standard boarding straight onto joists compresses insulation by ~50% and most people don't realise.

Building Regs?

Loft insulation needs to be 270 mm to meet current standards. I top up to that as standard. The boards, ladder and lights aren't notifiable but I do them to the same spec.

How much weight can the boards take?

Properly installed boarded loft holds 350 kg per square metre — heavy storage, christmas trees, suitcases, the lot. I'll show you the spec sheet.

Do you do the ladder too?

Yes — ladder, hatch, lights and boards are usually done as one job. Cheaper that way and tidier than getting separate trades in.
